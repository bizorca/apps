Loneliness among young urban professionals is well documented and poorly solved. Dating apps created the infrastructure for romantic connection and accidentally made platonic connection feel transactional. Networking events optimized for professional utility. The simple act of sitting down for a meal with interesting strangers, the social technology that has worked for centuries, never got a modern distribution layer.

Placecard matches compatible strangers for curated group dinners at local restaurants. A participant fills out a short profile covering age, interests, and dining preferences. The algorithm builds groups of four to six people likely to click, books a table at a partner restaurant, and handles payments and reminders. Restaurants fill slow-night tables with groups who linger and spend. Participants get a real meal with interesting people and no agenda beyond the food.

Start with one city and 3 partner restaurants before building anything beyond a booking system. Run the first 10 dinners manually: make the matches by hand, send the reminders personally, show up to observe. The data from those dinners, who stays late, which group configurations work, what the conversation looks like when it lands, shapes every product decision that follows. The honest structural challenge is safety: solo diners, particularly women, need to trust the vetting process before they show up. Address that in the onboarding before the matching algorithm gets any attention. A participant who feels safe enough to return is the only validation signal that matters at this stage.

Placecard charges nothing per seat for first-time diners and nothing for a weekly dinner subscription. Partner restaurants pay nothing and gain guaranteed covers on slow nights, that dynamic makes outreach straightforward. Corporate team events and company offsites are the natural expansion path: same format, higher headcount, existing budget. The dinner builds the trust.
