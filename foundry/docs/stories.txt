  The Landing Page
                  
  Before I build it, I want to push back on one assumption: the process and 
  deliverables are not what converts a visitor into an applicant. They matter,  
  but they're secondary. What converts is resonance with a specific problem the 
  person already knows they have. The E-Myth problem is visceral — you built a  
  business and it just became a job you can't quit. That's the hook.         

  The page structure I'd build:                                                 
  
  1. Hero — A single sharp statement about the problem. Not "here's what I do," 
  but "here's the trap you're in."                                           
  2. The Problem (E-Myth in plain English) — Most businesses fail the owner, not
   because they fail. They succeed at the work and fail as businesses. The owner
   IS the business.
  3. The Solution — Building the franchise prototype: a business that works     
  without you. Documented systems, defined roles, repeatable processes.         
  4. Why This Is Different — The combination nobody else brings: built and sold
  businesses, turned around hundreds of small ones, invested in 30 startups with
   full due diligence, plus accounting/tax/marketing, plus the ability to    
  actually build the software tools that implement the systems.                 
  5. The Process — Four phases: Audit → Architecture → Build → Handoff       
  6. The Deliverables — Concrete list. Not "strategies and frameworks" — actual 
  outputs.                                                                      
  7. Who This Is For / Who It Isn't — Application-only for a reason.            
  8. Apply CTA
