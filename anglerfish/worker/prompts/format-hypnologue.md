# Format: the Hypnologue essay

One shape, used every time. It is deliberately looser than the Bizorca weekly
post — this publication runs on argument rather than on deliverables — but it is
not formless, and the four movements below are load-bearing.

## The four movements

**1. The claim, concretely.** Open on the specific thing: a demonstration that
works, a line somebody famous wrote, a technique that should not work and does,
or an admission that the obvious reading is wrong. One or two paragraphs. No
definition-first openings, no "for centuries, humans have wondered."

**2. Why the obvious reading is incomplete.** State the version most people
hold, fairly — a straw man here is worse than useless, because the sceptical
reader is holding the strong version. Then show where it does not hold.

**3. The evidence, and what it actually says.** This is the movement the whole
publication exists for. Name the study, name the researchers, say what they
actually did and what actually came out. Report the shape of the finding, not
just its headline: how many participants, what the comparison was, what the
result was measured against.

Where a study's own abstract does not give you a number, do not supply one. Say
what it reported in the words it reported it — "over half", "significantly
greater than the control" — and leave the precision where the source left it.

If the best evidence **disagrees** with the claim in movement 1, that is not a
problem to route around. That is the post, and it is a better post. Say so
plainly.

**4. What follows.** The implication, in two or three sentences. Not a summary,
not a call to action, not a list of takeaways. The idea the reader carries out.

## References

Every post ends with a `References` heading and the sources listed in APA form
with a DOI. Nothing else goes under that heading — no further reading, no
"see also", no links to training courses.

```
References

Gorassini, D. R., & Spanos, N. P. (1986). A social-cognitive skills approach to
the successful modification of hypnotic susceptibility. Journal of Personality
and Social Psychology, 50(5), 1004–1012. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.50.5.1004
```

Every DOI is fetched from CrossRef before this post can be promoted. See the
evidence section of the brief for what to do when you are not certain of one —
the short version is that `[DOI: unverified]` and `[NO SOURCE FOUND: ...]` are
both correct answers and a guessed DOI is not.

## Things this format does not have

- A Go! section. That is a Bizorca device and it does not transfer.
- A downloadable deliverable, a worksheet, a checklist or a scored test.
- A curriculum module. Posts here are not slotted against a syllabus.
- Numbered takeaways or a summary box.
- A subscribe prompt, an upsell, or a mention of any of Jassen's tools. Kokoro
  and TaxCRM belong in Bizorca Press; they have no place in this publication.

## Titles

Short, concrete, no colon-subtitle construction unless it genuinely earns it. The
title names the subject; it does not summarise the conclusion. "Backwards Into
Trance" is a title. "The Surprising Science of Hypnotic Induction: What Research
Reveals" is not.

The `SUBTITLE:` line is one plain sentence saying what the post examines. It is
not a teaser and it does not end in an ellipsis.

## Length

200 to 700 words of body, excluding the References block, unless the brief asks
for something else. Shorter is normal here. Do not pad.
