## The twelve modules

One system installed per module. The client isn't buying calls — they're buying twelve
documented systems and the accountability to actually install them. Each module produces
an artifact that lives in the business afterward.

**The ordering is by dependency, not by domain.** Module 8 works because module 1
happened. Never write a post that assumes a later module's work has already been done.

### Module 1 — Money Baseline

You can't fix what you can't see, and most owners have never seen it. Establishes the
true financial picture before any decisions get made against it.

- Separate personal from business, if that hasn't happened
- Establish the cash position and the fixed monthly burn
- Calculate break-even honestly, including the owner's actual time
- Determine what the owner is really taking home, and what they need to

**Artifact:** A one-page financial baseline — cash position, break-even, true owner pay.

### Module 2 — The Owner's Time Audit

Gerber's three roles, applied to an actual week. Most owners discover they're spending
eighty percent of their time as technician and calling it running a business.

- Track two weeks of real time, not remembered time
- Sort every hour into technician, manager, or entrepreneur
- Identify the highest-value hour and the lowest
- Name what only the owner can do — and what they've simply never handed off

**Artifact:** The time audit, with the three-way split and the first candidate for removal.

### Module 3 — The One Offer

Most marketing problems are offer problems. Narrows the offer to something describable in
a sentence and priced against real delivery cost.

- Pick the single service that produces the most value and the least friction
- Write it as the buyer would describe it, not as the industry does
- Set the price against actual capacity and overhead, not against what competitors charge
- Decide what gets declined — an offer without a boundary isn't one

**Artifact:** The written offer and the pricing rationale behind it.

### Module 4 — Lead Flow

One channel, built to work, rather than five channels dabbled in. The channel is chosen by
a question, not by preference: does the buyer know they have this problem before they meet
you?

- **No** → workshop-led. The teaching does the diagnosis and the room self-selects.
- **Yes** → referral-led. Three source types: professionals who see the problem first,
  employers via a concierge-benefit structure, and trade associations.
- Build one, measure it, and leave the others alone until this one produces

**Artifact:** One documented acquisition channel with a defined weekly action.

These two modalities are the program's teaching examples throughout. Posts may go deep on
either. Do not introduce a third channel as a peer to these two.

### Module 5 — The Sales Conversation

The gap between an interested prospect and a paying client is usually a conversation
nobody planned. Makes it repeatable.

- Map the conversation from first contact to signed agreement
- Write the questions that qualify, and the ones that disqualify
- Handle the price objection before it arrives
- Define the follow-up sequence, since most sales are lost to silence rather than to no

**Artifact:** A documented conversation structure and a written follow-up sequence.

### Module 6 — Delivery Documented

The franchise prototype question, made concrete: if you handed this to someone else next
month, what would they need?

- Write the delivery process step by step, in the order it happens
- Capture the decisions the owner makes without thinking — hardest to hand off, most
  valuable to record
- Note the tools, templates, and checkpoints at each stage
- Build it as a working document, not a manual nobody opens

**Artifact:** The task library — the delivery process written down.

### Module 7 — Collections Cadence

Revenue that never arrives isn't revenue. Short on theory and long on the specific
discomfort of asking for money.

- Set invoice timing so it isn't a decision each time
- Write the follow-up sequence — first notice, second, and the call
- Set terms the owner will actually enforce, not aspirational ones
- Work the current aging list before the month is out

**Artifact:** The collections calendar and a cleared backlog.

### Module 8 — The Price Increase

Executed, not contemplated. This is where the earlier work pays for itself, and where
behavioral resistance shows up most reliably.

- Set the new price against the module 3 rationale and the module 1 numbers
- Decide who it applies to and when — new clients only, or everyone at renewal
- Write the client communication before sending it
- Send it

**Artifact:** The increase in effect, with the communication sent.

### Module 9 — First Delegation

One role, defined and handed off. Not a hiring plan — a single transfer that proves the
task library works.

- Pick the task with the highest hours and the lowest required judgment
- Define the role against what module 6 documented
- Establish what "done" looks like and how it gets checked
- Hand it over and stop doing it

**Artifact:** One role defined, documented, and transferred.

This module is the natural home for role-document deliverables, but position contracts can
appear anywhere a role is being defined.

### Module 10 — The Owner's Dashboard

Five numbers, reviewed weekly. The distinction that matters: tax-return accounting is
historical, and operating decisions are forward-looking.

- Trailing 90-day cash flow
- Fixed monthly burn
- Pipeline or booked work ahead
- Receivables aging
- One number specific to this business

**Artifact:** The dashboard, with a standing weekly review on the calendar.

### Module 11 — Tax and Entity Hygiene

The regulatory layer, addressed once a year rather than discovered in a letter. This is
where the EA credential does work no other coach in this price band can do.

- Review entity structure against current stage — most owners are in the wrong one for
  where they are now
- Set the estimated payment schedule and fund it from module 1's allocation
- Check worker classification, B&O classification, and licensing
- Identify what belongs with a CPA before year-end rather than after

**Artifact:** A structure review and a funded quarterly schedule.

Washington State specifics (B&O, licensing) are in scope and are a differentiator. Posts
in this module should stay on the EA side of the line — tax and entity structure, not
investment advice.

### Module 12 — The Annual Plan

One page. Everything installed across the year, turned into what happens next.

- Name the one constraint that limits growth right now
- Set three targets with dates and numbers attached
- Decide what gets stopped — a plan without subtraction is a wish list
- Schedule the quarterly reviews that keep it alive

**Artifact:** The next twelve months on a single page.

## How the twelve split across cohorts

The twelve modules are delivered as two cohort programs, not one.

**Foundations — 12 weeks.** Modules 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10. Money baseline, time audit, the
offer, lead flow, the sales conversation, collections, the owner's dashboard. Teaches
things owners don't know. Sessions are lecture-plus-worksheet.

**Growth — 16 weeks.** Modules 6, 8, 9, 11, 12. Delivery documentation, the price increase,
first delegation, entity and tax hygiene, the annual plan. Draws from Foundations
graduates. Makes owners do things they're avoiding — roughly half the sessions are work
review rather than new teaching, and the pressure is the product.

Confirmed Growth pacing: weeks 7–9 the price increase, weeks 10–13 first delegation, week
14 entity and tax structure taught once, weeks 15–16 the annual plan and the close. Earlier
weeks cover delivery documentation and are mostly working sessions.

**Calendar.** Three Foundations cohorts a year targeting February, June, and October
starts. Growth placed January 2028 for the first run. The third Foundations cohort is the
Zoom recording cohort, with teaching segments recorded separately from live sessions.

Post sequence follows module number 1–12, not cohort order. A reader is not enrolled in
anything.

## Where readers stall

Modules 3, 8, and 9 — the offer, the price increase, the delegation — are where clients
reliably stall. The obstacle there is behavioral, not informational.

Posts in those modules should assume the reader already knows what to do and hasn't done
it. The trap is avoidance, the diagnostic surfaces the avoidance, and the protocol removes
the decision rather than explaining the concept again.

This is the only place resistance and money-behavior material belongs. Keep it scoped to
business behavior — resistance to marketing, to raising prices, to implementing systems.
Not therapeutic content.

---

## Audience translation

The curriculum was written for owner-operators in the $60–100K range on the Olympic
Peninsula. The Substack audience is practitioners: health and wellness providers, coaches,
consultants, tax professionals.

Translate every module into practice terms. Module 9 is not a warehouse hire — it's the
front-desk role a solo massage therapist hasn't defined yet. Module 7 is not net-30
invoicing — it's the client who no-showed and still owes for the package. Module 11 is not
a corporate reorg — it's whether an acupuncturist should still be a sole proprietor.
